News · African Grand Prix

F1 is going back to Africa: Rwanda signs a 10-year deal for an African Grand Prix

Formula 1 has agreed a 10-year deal with Rwanda to stage the African Grand Prix, the sport's first race on the continent since the 1993 South African Grand Prix at Kyalami. It was announced in Kigali on Tuesday, with President Paul Kagame and F1 chief executive Stefano Domenicali.

There is no date yet. The race is aimed at the early 2030s, 2030 at the earliest, because the circuit still has to be built: a new permanent road course at Bugesera, about 40 km south of Kigali, next to the country's new international airport, which is due to open in the second half of 2028.

Carsten Tilke and former F1 driver Alex Wurz have designed a layout of around 6 km with 17 corners, run anti-clockwise between two hills that form a natural grandstand and alongside a lake. Wurz called it a roller coaster: the lap rises and falls 197 metres in total, almost twice Spa-Francorchamps' 102, and it includes a kilometre-long back straight into a hairpin. The design is finished and now moves into engineering.

33 years away

F1's last race in Africa was the 1993 South African Grand Prix at Kyalami. Rwanda formally bid to bring it back in December 2024.

Built for the hills

A natural road course rather than a street circuit, with 197 metres of elevation change a lap and a long back straight that sets up an overtaking spot into the hairpin.

Still to come

The first race date, the final track length (reports range from 5.3 to about 6 km) and the construction milestones will be announced later, F1 says.

No circuit drawing yet: the track is not built and no official layout data has been published, so we will add one when it is.

Recap · Round 20 · São Paulo

Norris wins a wet-dry-wet Interlagos as Verstappen charges from 21st to second

Lando Norris converted his rain-soaked pole into victory at Interlagos, 14.6 seconds clear, his third win of the season. Behind him, Max Verstappen started 21st after a power-unit penalty and carved through the field to second, with Isack Hadjar third for a Red Bull double podium.

George Russell's fourth and Kimi Antonelli's fifth gave Mercedes the points they needed: they are the 2026 Constructors' Champions, completing the double with Antonelli's drivers' title.

SÃO PAULO GP · INTERLAGOS · NOV 8, 2026

Antonelli led the early laps from second on the grid, but spun at Turn 1 around lap 20 and handed the lead to Norris; he later reported a brake problem. Verstappen passed the struggling Mercedes on lap 24 for third and kept climbing as the conditions changed, with most of the front-runners stopping three times.

Oscar Piastri was sixth and Liam Lawson seventh. Both Ferraris started from the back half of the grid after engine penalties: Charles Leclerc came through to eighth despite a five-second penalty for causing a collision, and Lewis Hamilton went from 20th to ninth. Nico Hülkenberg took the final point. Lance Stroll retired with a battery problem and Valtteri Bottas did not start after a cross-threaded wheel nut.

From 21st to second

Verstappen's recovery is the drive of the weekend, and it lifts him 17 points clear in the fight for second.

The double

Mercedes have both titles: Antonelli's drivers' crown in Mexico and the constructors' at Interlagos, 142 points clear of Ferrari.

Looking ahead

Las Vegas is next, on Saturday 21 November: a night race down the Strip, with three Grands Prix and 75 points left.

Champions · Mercedes

Mercedes complete the double as the constructors' title is sealed

Needing only a handful of points in Brazil, Mercedes took 22 with Russell fourth and Antonelli fifth. They lead Ferrari by 142 points with 129 left, so the Constructors' Championship is theirs to go with Antonelli's drivers' title.

Red Bull's double podium moves them 17 points clear of McLaren in the fight for third.

Las Vegas Grand Prix — Race Preview

Round 21 · Las Vegas Strip Circuit · Nov 19–21

Both championships are decided, but the fight to be runner-up is wide open. Max Verstappen leads it on 279 after his drive from 21st to second in Brazil, ahead of George Russell (262), Lewis Hamilton (261), Charles Leclerc (249) and Lando Norris (246), with three Grands Prix and 75 points left.

Las Vegas runs on Saturday night: practice on Thursday, qualifying on Friday at 8pm and the 50-lap race on Saturday 21 November at 8pm local time. Pirelli has brought its softest three compounds to every Las Vegas race so far, and grip on a cold, little-used street surface is always the question.

Tyre compounds

The Las Vegas Strip Circuit runs 6.201 km anticlockwise over 17 corners, past the Sphere and the casinos. The heart of the lap is the run from Turn 12 down Las Vegas Boulevard to the Turn 14 chicane, more than 1.9 km taken completely flat. Desert night temperatures can drop close to 10°C, which makes warming the tyres the hardest job of the weekend.

Fight for second

Verstappen 279, Russell 262, Hamilton 261, Leclerc 249, Norris 246: five drivers, 33 points, 75 left.

Form guide

Verstappen has won two of the three Las Vegas races (2023 and 2025); Russell won in 2024 from pole.

Strategy

Last year the whole top 10 stopped once; in 2024 almost everyone stopped twice. Cold tyres and graining decide which.
