News · African Grand Prix

F1 is going back to Africa: Rwanda signs a 10-year deal for an African Grand Prix

Formula 1 has agreed a 10-year deal with Rwanda to stage the African Grand Prix, the sport's first race on the continent since the 1993 South African Grand Prix at Kyalami. It was announced in Kigali on Tuesday, with President Paul Kagame and F1 chief executive Stefano Domenicali.

There is no date yet. The race is aimed at the early 2030s, 2030 at the earliest, because the circuit still has to be built: a new permanent road course at Bugesera, about 40 km south of Kigali, next to the country's new international airport, which is due to open in the second half of 2028.

Carsten Tilke and former F1 driver Alex Wurz have designed a layout of around 6 km with 17 corners, run anti-clockwise between two hills that form a natural grandstand and alongside a lake. Wurz called it a roller coaster: the lap rises and falls 197 metres in total, almost twice Spa-Francorchamps' 102, and it includes a kilometre-long back straight into a hairpin. The design is finished and now moves into engineering.

33 years away

F1's last race in Africa was the 1993 South African Grand Prix at Kyalami. Rwanda formally bid to bring it back in December 2024.

Built for the hills

A natural road course rather than a street circuit, with 197 metres of elevation change a lap and a long back straight that sets up an overtaking spot into the hairpin.

Still to come

The first race date, the final track length (reports range from 5.3 to about 6 km) and the construction milestones will be announced later, F1 says.

No circuit drawing yet: the track is not built and no official layout data has been published, so we will add one when it is.

Champion · Round 19 · Mexico City

Antonelli is world champion, the youngest in Formula 1 history

Kimi Antonelli finished third in Mexico City to clinch the 2026 title with four rounds to spare. At 20 years and 68 days he is the youngest champion in the sport's history, more than three years younger than Sebastian Vettel in 2010, and Italy's first world champion since Alberto Ascari in 1953.

Max Verstappen won the race from fifth on the grid, his sixth Mexico City victory, holding off Lewis Hamilton by 0.799 seconds after a late restart.

MEXICO CITY GP · AUTÓDROMO HERMANOS RODRÍGUEZ · NOV 1, 2026

Hamilton started from his first pole for Ferrari, but he and Charles Leclerc ran each other off the road on the opening lap and Leclerc was given a five-second penalty. Verstappen made his only stop on lap 27 and came out ahead of Leclerc, who served his penalty at the same stop.

On lap 44 Valtteri Bottas's Cadillac suffered a front-left suspension failure and crashed heavily at Turn 1. He escaped with a bruised knee, and the race was red-flagged with the barriers badly damaged. At the standing restart, with 25 laps to go, Hamilton jumped Leclerc for second and attacked Verstappen into Turn 1, but the Red Bull held on. Antonelli brought his Mercedes home third, Leclerc fourth, Lando Norris fifth and George Russell sixth.

Liam Lawson, disqualified from qualifying, recovered from a pit-lane start to seventh. Gabriel Bortoleto was eighth, Isack Hadjar ninth, and Ryō Hirakawa scored a point on his Formula 1 debut for Haas. Oscar Piastri and both Alpines were penalised after the race.

Youngest ever

20 years and 68 days old. Vettel, the previous youngest champion, was 23 when he won in 2010.

The fight for second

Verstappen's win lifts him to second on 261, two points ahead of Hamilton. Russell (250) and Leclerc (245) are still in it.

Looking ahead

Interlagos is next, a week later. Mercedes lead the Constructors' by 126 points, with up to 172 still available.

Debut · Haas

Hirakawa scores on his debut as Lawson recovers from the pit lane

Called up at short notice to replace Esteban Ocon at Haas, Ryō Hirakawa qualified 18th in his first Formula 1 session and finished tenth for a point on debut.

Liam Lawson was disqualified from qualifying for an MGU-K breach and started from the pit lane, then fought through to seventh for Racing Bulls, six points that keep them clear in fifth in the Constructors'.

São Paulo Grand Prix — Race Preview

Round 20 · Interlagos · Nov 6–8

Kimi Antonelli arrives at Interlagos as the 2026 World Champion. The title is settled, but the race to finish runner-up is not: Max Verstappen (261), Lewis Hamilton (259), George Russell (250) and Charles Leclerc (245) are separated by 16 points with four Grands Prix and 100 points left.

São Paulo hosts a standard weekend this year: three practice sessions, qualifying on Saturday and the 71-lap race on Sunday at 2pm local time. Pirelli brings the same softest trio as Mexico, one step softer than last year: the C3 as the hard, the C4 as the medium and the C5 as the soft.

Tyre compounds

The Autódromo José Carlos Pace runs 4.309 km anticlockwise over 15 turns, through the Senna S, down the Reta Oposta and round the twisting infield before the long uphill run past the pits. It first hosted the Brazilian Grand Prix in 1973 and has been home to the race every year since 1990, apart from 2020, and its weather is famously changeable.

Fight for second

Verstappen 261, Hamilton 259, Russell 250, Leclerc 245. Verstappen's Mexico win moved him from fourth to second.

Constructors'

Mercedes lead Ferrari by 126 with up to 172 points still available; one more strong weekend could settle it. Red Bull are 17 ahead of McLaren for third.

Form guide

Lando Norris won here last year from pole, with Antonelli second. Verstappen and Hamilton have three wins each at Interlagos.
