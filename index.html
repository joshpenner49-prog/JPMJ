Breaking · African Grand Prix

F1 is going back to Africa: Rwanda signs a 10-year deal for an African Grand Prix

Formula 1 has agreed a 10-year deal with Rwanda to stage the African Grand Prix, the sport's first race on the continent since the 1993 South African Grand Prix at Kyalami. It was announced in Kigali on Tuesday, with President Paul Kagame and F1 chief executive Stefano Domenicali.

There is no date yet. The race is aimed at the early 2030s, 2030 at the earliest, because the circuit still has to be built: a new permanent road course at Bugesera, about 40 km south of Kigali, next to the country's new international airport, which is due to open in the second half of 2028.

Carsten Tilke and former F1 driver Alex Wurz have designed a layout of around 6 km with 17 corners, run anti-clockwise between two hills that form a natural grandstand and alongside a lake. Wurz called it a roller coaster: the lap rises and falls 197 metres in total, almost twice Spa-Francorchamps' 102, and it includes a kilometre-long back straight into a hairpin. The design is finished and now moves into engineering.

33 years away

F1's last race in Africa was the 1993 South African Grand Prix at Kyalami. Rwanda formally bid to bring it back in December 2024.

Built for the hills

A natural road course rather than a street circuit, with 197 metres of elevation change a lap and a long back straight that sets up an overtaking spot into the hairpin.

Still to come

The first race date, the final track length (reports range from 5.3 to about 6 km) and the construction milestones will be announced later, F1 says.

No circuit drawing yet: the track is not built and no official layout data has been published, so we will add one when it is.

Recap · Round 17 · Singapore

Verstappen leads every lap from pole for his first Singapore Grand Prix win

Max Verstappen converted pole into a lights-to-flag victory at Marina Bay, his first win at the circuit and the 73rd of his career. He held off Charles Leclerc into the first corner, kept his rivals at arm's length through two Virtual Safety Car periods and took the flag 7.3 seconds clear, despite radio problems that left Red Bull relying on a pit board.

Leclerc finished second for Ferrari and Kimi Antonelli third for Mercedes, with Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri fourth and fifth for McLaren.

SINGAPORE GP · MARINA BAY · OCT 11, 2026

Lewis Hamilton started on mediums and was the first of the leaders to pit, at the end of lap 18, switching to softs and dropping from third to seventh. The call compromised his race, and he apologised for it afterwards; Piastri passed him late on and he finished sixth. Liam Lawson was seventh for Racing Bulls ahead of Nico Hülkenberg, while George Russell recovered from 19th on the grid, after an engine penalty, to ninth. Isack Hadjar took the final point.

Pierre Gasly, Oliver Bearman, Esteban Ocon, Carlos Sainz, Arvid Lindblad and Fernando Alonso finished 11th to 16th, a lap down, ahead of Sergio Pérez, Alex Albon, who was classified 18th after retiring late on, and Valtteri Bottas. Lance Stroll (suspected clutch), Franco Colapinto (driveline) and Gabriel Bortoleto (gearbox) retired.

Lap 18

Hamilton's early switch from mediums to softs dropped him from a podium place to seventh, and he never recovered the lost ground.

Championship shift

Antonelli's third place and Russell's two points stretch the lead to 102. Hamilton is now 9 points behind Russell in the fight for second.

Looking ahead

Two weeks until Austin, the first of the final six Grands Prix and the first weekend where Antonelli can clinch the title.

Sprint · Round 17 · Singapore

Verstappen wins a wet, chaotic Singapore Sprint as Russell crashes out of the lead

Torrential rain delayed Saturday's Sprint by 30 minutes, and George Russell's lead lasted barely a lap before he lost control on the kerb at the final corner and hit the wall. Verstappen, who started from pole, went on to win ahead of Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc.

Antonelli was fourth, ahead of Liam Lawson, Oliver Bearman, Nico Hülkenberg and Lando Norris, who kept the last point after tagging Oscar Piastri at Turn 5 on the final lap; Piastri hit the wall and retired. Lance Stroll and Sergio Pérez collided on the opening lap, Isack Hadjar and Franco Colapinto stopped with car problems behind the Safety Car, and Alex Albon, Gabriel Bortoleto and Valtteri Bottas were caught up in collisions.

United States Grand Prix — Race Preview

Round 18 · Circuit of the Americas · Oct 23–25

Kimi Antonelli leaves Singapore 102 points ahead of George Russell with six Grands Prix to go. Singapore was the last sprint of the season, so 150 points remain, and Austin is the first weekend where the championship can be settled.

The Circuit of the Americas hosts a standard weekend this year: three practice sessions, qualifying on Saturday and the 56-lap race on Sunday at 3pm local time. Last year's sprint here has moved elsewhere, and Pirelli has dropped last season's gap in its compound choice, bringing the C2, C3 and C4 as the hard, medium and soft.

Tyre compounds

The track runs 5.513 km over 20 turns, opening with the steep uphill climb into Turn 1, and has hosted the United States Grand Prix since 2012.

The title maths

With 150 points left, Antonelli is champion in Austin if he leaves with a lead of more than 125. Winning the race while Russell scores no more than a point and Hamilton finishes fifth or lower would do it.

Fight for second

Hamilton's sixth place and Russell's recovery to ninth closed the gap between them to 9 points, with Verstappen another 8 behind Hamilton after his Singapore double.

Red Bull's run

Verstappen has won the last two Grands Prix, at Sepang and Singapore, plus the Singapore Sprint. Red Bull are now 7 points behind McLaren for third in the Constructors' Championship.
