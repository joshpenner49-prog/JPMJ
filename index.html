Singapore Grand Prix — Race Preview

Round 17 · Marina Bay · Oct 9–11
Preview MARINA BAY · FIRST-EVER SPRINT WEEKEND · UNDER THE LIGHTS

F1's original night race adds a sprint as Antonelli closes in on the title

Antonelli's second place at Sepang, combined with Russell's late engine failure, stretched his lead to 84 points with seven rounds to go. Hamilton is now just 22 points behind Russell after recovering from near the back to the podium, so the fight for second is suddenly wide open.

Singapore completes the triple-header just a week after Sepang, and this year it hosts a sprint weekend for the first time: sprint qualifying on Friday night, the sprint and Grand Prix qualifying on Saturday, then the 62-lap race on Sunday at 8pm local time. Back in 2008, this was the first night race in F1 history.

The Marina Bay Street Circuit runs 4.927 km, with walls close at almost every corner, so Safety Cars are common and track position is everything. With the sprint format, teams get just one practice session to set up their cars for one of the hottest, most humid weekends of the year.

Extra points on Saturday

The sprint puts more points on the table than a normal weekend, which helps anyone chasing, but also gives Antonelli another chance to extend his lead.

Russell's last stand

Still mathematically alive, but 84 points back. He needs a big weekend and a bad one for Antonelli, and he needs both soon.

Can Verstappen go back-to-back?

After a double podium in Baku and a win at Sepang, Red Bull arrive with real momentum. Pole matters even more around Marina Bay than most tracks.

Breaking Motorsport News

Filed Oct 4, 2026
Breaking SEPANG · TITLE FIGHT

Russell's engine gives out with five laps to go, and his title hopes go with it

George Russell was running third under a late Safety Car when his Mercedes power unit failed on the home straight with five laps remaining. A near-certain podium became zero points, and with Kimi Antonelli finishing second, the gap between the Mercedes team-mates jumped from 66 to 84 points in a single afternoon.

Russell admitted afterwards that his title chances are now very unlikely, and team boss Toto Wolff made no secret of his frustration with the failure.

A Sepang echo

Ten years ago at this same circuit, Lewis Hamilton's Mercedes engine failed while he was leading the 2016 Malaysian Grand Prix, a blow widely seen as costing him that year's title to team-mate Nico Rosberg.

A new fight for second

Hamilton's podium leaves him just 22 points behind Russell, turning second place in the championship into a genuine battle with seven rounds to go.

What it means for Antonelli

An 84-point lead with seven rounds left is more than three race wins' worth of cushion. Russell needs Antonelli to stumble, and soon.

Bahrain Grand Prix — Race Recap

Round 16 · Sepang, Malaysia · Complete
Recap SEPANG · VERSTAPPEN WINS

Verstappen wins a rain-delayed Sepang thriller for his first victory of 2026

Torrential rain pushed the start back by an hour and 40 minutes, and the first attempt to get going was abandoned when cars hit engine-software problems and had to reboot on track. When the race finally began, the field was split between slicks and intermediates, and the intermediate runners were far quicker early on. Pole-sitter Max Verstappen lost the lead to both Mercedes off the start, got back past them either side of an early Safety Car, then simply drove away.

Kimi Antonelli finished second, 2.3 seconds behind, with Lewis Hamilton third. Both Ferraris had tumbled down the order on slicks in the opening laps. Hamilton was the only driver not to pit under the lap-9 Safety Car for Valtteri Bottas's spin, and he climbed back through the field. A late Safety Car for Alex Albon's stopped Williams bunched everyone up, and Hamilton passed Isack Hadjar for third with three laps left, with Charles Leclerc following him through for fourth. Hadjar, Oscar Piastri and Liam Lawson completed the top seven. Fernando Alonso took eighth, Aston Martin's best result of the season, holding off Lando Norris, who had slid into the gravel under the Safety Car. Arvid Lindblad took the last point. George Russell, Albon and Bottas retired, and the race ran 55 laps instead of the planned 56.

Strategy note

The starting tyre decided most of the race. Slick starters lost huge chunks of time, and the lap-9 Safety Car arrived right at the crossover point for everyone else to switch.

Championship shift

Antonelli's second place and Russell's retirement push the gap at the top to 84 points, with Hamilton now just 22 behind Russell in the fight for second.

Looking ahead

Singapore completes the triple-header next weekend, and for the first time it's a sprint weekend under the Marina Bay lights.
