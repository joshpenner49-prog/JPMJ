Two Straight Mode zones: the start/finish straight into Turn 1 and the Reta Oposta from Turn 3 to Turn 4. Overtake Mode detection is at the exit of Junção, with activation on the climb after Turn 13.
The Senna SAnalysisTurn 1 drops sharply downhill and left, straight into the right of Turn 2. It is the main overtaking spot at the start of a lap and the scene of many first-lap collisions.
Reta OpostaAnalysisAfter Curva do Sol, the back straight runs down to Turn 4, Descida do Lago: the second big braking zone and the next chance to pass.
The infieldAnalysisA slow, twisting run of corners through the middle of the lap. It is hard to pass here, so drivers aim for a clean exit before the climb.
Junção and the climbAnalysisJunção, the last slow corner, opens a long, flat-out uphill run past the pits to Turn 1. A good exit is worth a tow all the way to the Senna S.
The lap hinges on Junção: get out of it well and the long climb carries the car into the Senna S with a chance to pass.Analysis

What a pit stop really costs

Time in the pit lane is not the same as time lost. What matters is the race time a stop costs compared with staying out, and that changes when the race is neutralised.

ConditionsTime in pit laneNet race-time lossSaving vs green
Green flagabout 24 sMeasuredabout 21 sEstimate—
Virtual Safety Carabout 24 sMeasuredabout 13–17 sEstimateabout 4–8 s
Safety Carabout 24 sMeasuredabout 8–14 sEstimateabout 7–12 s
  • Time in the pit lane is measured: the median from pit entry to pit exit at Interlagos was 23.2–24.0 s in 2019, 2022, 2023 and 2025 (f1db timing data); the wet, red-flagged 2024 race skews it. It does not change under a VSC or Safety Car because the pit-lane speed limit still applies.
  • Net race-time loss is what a stop costs compared with staying out. Formula 1's 2025 preview put it at about 20.8 s here.
  • The VSC and Safety Car figures apply published estimates of the saving (roughly 17–40% under a VSC and 35–60% behind the Safety Car) to that 20.8 s. They are illustrative ranges, not Interlagos measurements.

Tyre strategyVerified

Pirelli's projection after qualifying: two stops is the quickest plan, with two soft stints and one on the medium. A soft-hard-soft race is slower, and one stop is about ten seconds slower than the best two-stop. Pirelli puts a stop at 22.5 seconds. Rain would rewrite all of it.

Five things to watch

  1. The start into the Senna S, downhill and left: the best chance of the lap to gain places, and the riskiest.
  2. The fight for second. Two points split Verstappen and Hamilton, with Russell and Leclerc within 16.
  3. Mercedes' Constructors' title. Outscore Ferrari by four points or more and it is settled with three races to spare.
  4. Safety Cars and weather. Formula 1 puts the Safety Car chance at 86%; watch the skies above the grandstands.
  5. Antonelli's first race as champion, at the track where he took second place last year.
JP's predictionInterlagos never sticks to the script, and this year the chaos started before the lights: rain in the forecast, Leclerc down in 14th and Hamilton, Verstappen and Pérez at the back. As a Ferrari fan it hurts, but it sets up a great race. I have Lando Norris converting pole into the win, with Max Verstappen charging from the back onto the podium.— JP
