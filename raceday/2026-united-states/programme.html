The title can be won in Texas

Sunday 25 October 2026Circuit of The Americas · Austin, TexasRace 15:00 CDT

Kimi Antonelli arrives at the Circuit of The Americas 102 points clear with six Grands Prix left. For the first time this season, a single Sunday could settle the championship.

Singapore was the last sprint of 2026, so from here every point comes on a Sunday: 25 for a win, six races, 150 points in total. Antonelli's lead is 102. Behind him, three drivers are covered by 17 points in the fight for second, and Red Bull have closed to within seven points of McLaren for third in the Constructors' Championship. Austin is a standard weekend, with three practice sessions, qualifying on Saturday and the 56-lap race on Sunday.

Two Straight Mode zones: the back straight between Turns 11 and 12, and the start/finish straight from Turn 20 towards Turn 1. Overtake Mode detection is just after Turn 20: a car within one second of the car ahead there gets extra electrical energy to attack.
Turn 1VerifiedThe start straight climbs steeply, at around an 11% gradient, into a left-hand hairpin: one of the steepest uphill braking zones in F1. The wide entry allows several lines on the opening lap.
The essesAnalysisA fast sequence of direction changes after Turn 2. Rhythm matters more than any single apex: one late turn-in costs time through every corner that follows.
Turns 11 and 12VerifiedTurn 11 is the slowest corner on the lap and feeds the longest straight, so traction on exit decides the run to Turn 12, the clearest overtaking spot on the track.
Stadium and multi-apexAnalysisTight corners in the stadium section, then a long multi-apex right-hander that keeps the car loaded for several seconds. A car that slides here wears its tyres faster over a stint.
Most lap time is won or lost on corner exits that lead onto straights: Turn 11 onto the back straight and the final corner onto the start/finish straight. A poor exit costs time all the way to the next braking zone, and gives the car behind its best chance to attack.Analysis

What a pit stop really costs

Time in the pit lane is not the same as time lost. What matters is the race time a stop costs compared with staying out, and that changes when the race is neutralised.

ConditionsTime in pit laneNet race-time lossSaving vs green

  • Time in the pit lane is measured: the median from pit entry to pit exit at COTA was 24.0–24.6 s in every race from 2019 to 2025 (f1db timing data). It includes the stationary tyre change of a few seconds, and it does not change under a VSC or Safety Car because the pit-lane speed limit still applies.
  • Net race-time loss is what a stop costs compared with staying out. Pirelli put the total time cost of a stop at COTA at about 20.5 s under green flag (2024 preview).
  • The VSC and Safety Car figures apply published estimates of the saving (roughly 17–40% under a VSC and 35–60% behind the Safety Car) to that 20.5 s. They are illustrative ranges, not COTA measurements: the real gain depends on where each car is when the period starts and how long it lasts.

Undercut and overcut

Undercut: pit first and use the new tyres' extra grip to gain the time you lose in the pit lane before your rival stops. It works best when new tyres are much faster than worn ones.

Overcut: stay out longer and gain time on a clear track while your rival is held up on cold new tyres or in traffic. It works best when tyres warm up slowly or the pitting car rejoins in traffic.

If the VSC or Safety Car comes out

A VSC or Safety Car inside the pit window makes a stop cheaper for everyone, so the leaders often pit together and little changes. The real swing comes when it falls just before a team's planned stop, or when a rival has already stopped under green. A Safety Car early in the race can make a one-stop too long and force a second stop.Analysis

What to Watch

  1. Who stops first. On a one-stop race, an early stop is either an undercut attempt or a sign that tyre wear is higher than expected.
  2. Lap 20 onwards. That is when the one-stop window opened in each of the last two races.
  3. Any VSC between laps 20 and 40: watch who reacts and who stays out.

Five things to watch

  1. The run to Turn 1. The climb and the wide hairpin make the first corner a lottery: watch who goes around the outside and who is squeezed on the exit.
  2. The title maths. To be champion on Sunday, Antonelli must outscore Russell by at least 24 points, Hamilton by 15, Verstappen by 7 and Leclerc by 1. Watch where Russell and Hamilton are running at half distance.
  3. The Turn 11 exit. Traction onto the longest straight decides who can attack into Turn 12, where most passes happen.
  4. Laps 20 to 40. The one-stop window in the last two races. The first front-runner to pit tells you what the tyres are doing.
  5. Verstappen chasing a third straight Grand Prix win, and Red Bull trying to overtake McLaren for third in the Constructors'.
